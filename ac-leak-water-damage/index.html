How an air conditioner ends up leaking inside the house

Every cooling system pulls water out of the air, and that condensate has to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, cracks or sags, the drain pan fills and overflows. An air handler in an attic or a closet then drips onto drywall, insulation and flooring.

Other sources are an iced evaporator coil that melts when the unit shuts off, a rusted secondary pan, and a wall mounted mini-split with a blocked drain. Texas summers run these systems for months, so a small blockage turns into hundreds of hours of slow dripping.

Ceiling stain under the attic, wet closet floor, drip from the vent

People notice a faint brown ring on the hallway ceiling, a soft spot in carpet near the air handler closet, or water dripping around a vent. Some hear a gurgle or find the pan full of slime. These signs describe slow water damage, and the trade term is drying and restoration of the structure the condensate reached.

AC leak safety: turn the system off first

Turn the system off at the thermostat. If the air handler sits in a dry spot you can reach, switch off its breaker as well. Running the unit while the drain is blocked keeps adding water.

Water, wiring and sagging ceilings

Air handlers carry electrical parts, so do not touch the unit or the wiring if water is pooled around it. Stay out from under a ceiling that sags or bulges, because wet drywall can fall. If you smell burning or gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.

Wet attic insulation is heavy and hides the joists. Do not walk across it.

How we dry and restore after an AC drain failure

We start with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find where the condensate went. Sections of wet insulation are removed. Ceiling board that has softened is cut out in a controlled way, and wet baseboards or carpet pad near a closet are lifted.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the joists, subfloor and wall bottoms. Readings are logged daily until the framing reaches dry standards, and crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, a published industry reference for water damage work. Because AC leaks repeat, we also note where the pan, line and unit sit so you can pass that to the HVAC technician.

We do not repair air conditioners. That belongs to an HVAC company, and clearing the drain before drying avoids the same flood twice. After the structure is dry, new drywall and paint fall under water damage repair.

Slow leaks, insurance and the mold that follows

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says policies usually exclude damage from gradual leaks and the mold that results, while sudden, accidental water is often covered. AC condensate often falls on the gradual side, because it drips for weeks before anyone sees it. Check your policy and ask your insurer how it treats your case.

The same agency notes that mold can begin to grow inside 24 hours of a leak. That is why a ceiling that has dripped for a season deserves fast drying. If growth is visible after repeated drips, our mold remediation page explains the next step.

Prevention: keep the drain from failing twice

  • Ask an HVAC technician to flush the condensate line each year.
  • Check that the secondary pan is dry and not rusted through.
  • Install a float switch that shuts the system off when the pan fills.
  • Look at the ceiling beneath the attic unit every season.
  • Do not run a dripping system while you wait for repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you offer same-day response for an AC leak in Little Elm?

Yes, we try to book a visit the same day, with 24/7 dispatch for after-hours calls. Shut the system off first so the leak stops growing.

How can I tell condensate from a roof leak?

Watch the timing. Condensate drips only while the AC runs and tends to appear near the air handler, while a roof leak shows up after rain even with the AC off.

Does a clogged AC drain count as sudden damage?

It depends on the policy and the facts. Texas Department of Insurance says gradual leaks are usually excluded, so ask your insurer how it classifies the event.

Can you fix the air conditioner?

No. An HVAC technician repairs the unit, the drain and the pan. We dry the wet ceiling, insulation and framing.

Is my attic insulation ruined?

Soaked insulation is usually removed. Meter readings decide whether lightly damp sections can dry in place.

What if the ceiling stain keeps coming back?

A returning stain means moisture is still reaching it or the framing never fully dried. Call us, and have the drain line checked at the same time.
