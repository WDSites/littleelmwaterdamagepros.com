A community built one phase at a time

The Town of Little Elm lists the Sunset Pointe HOA and a separate Sunset Place HOA in its HOA directory, in the same Town zone as the Frisco Ranch and Frisco Hills associations. Listing records for Sunset Pointe run through at least a Phase 14, and listing sites show both detached homes and townhomes.

Phases mean different construction years. We have not verified build years for individual sections, so ask your HOA or your closing paperwork when your section was finished. That date tells you how old the water heater, the supply lines and the dishwasher hose may be.

Where the school district ends and the water bill begins

Wikipedia notes that Sunset Pointe and Frisco Ranch are partly served by Frisco ISD schools, even though the homes are in the Town of Little Elm. Signs that water is where you cannot see it

Swollen cabinet toe-kicks, a floor that feels soft near the refrigerator, a closet wall that is cold to the touch and a ceiling stain under a bathroom point to a slow leak or a failed fitting. These are moisture problems with a hidden path. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show how far the water has gone before anything is torn out.

Townhome and shared-wall questions

Listing sites show townhomes in Sunset Pointe, and water in an attached home can cross a shared wall. Tell your neighbor and your HOA early. Ask your association what its master policy covers and what is left to your own policy, because the answer differs from one townhome community to the next.

First hour safety steps

Stop the source

Turn the appliance valve or the main valve off if you can reach it dry. A water heater leak means turning off its power or gas control, not touching a wet electrical connection.

Keep power away from water

Stay away from the panel unless it is dry and within easy reach. Do not unplug anything that stands in water.

Look at the water

Cloudy, dark or smelly water may carry waste. Treat it as contaminated and keep everyone out.

Lift what you can

Raise small furniture, rugs and electronics off wet flooring if it is safe to do so.

How the cleanup and drying work

Cleanup starts with extraction, then removal of materials that will not dry in place, such as saturated pad or crumbling drywall. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the structure while moisture readings are logged against ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard. A clean line break sits in Category 1 and dishwasher or washer discharge sits in Category 2, which changes how much comes out.

Our water damage cleanup page walks through the stages. Appliance and tank failures are covered on water heater leak cleanup, and wet cabinets and floors on kitchen water damage restoration.

Insurance records

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that sudden, accidental water such as a failed supply line is usually covered, while gradual seepage usually is not. Take photos, list what was affected, save receipts and set damaged items aside for the adjuster, and hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has seen the damage. Whether a claim is covered depends on the cause and your policy, so ask your insurer.

FEMA flood-zone status for Sunset Pointe as a whole is not something we have verified, and the FEMA map works address by address, so look yours up if flooding is a worry.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sunset Pointe really in Little Elm?

Yes. The Town of Little Elm lists the Sunset Pointe HOA in its HOA directory. Part of the community is served by Frisco ISD schools, which is a school boundary and not a city boundary.

How old are the homes in Sunset Pointe?

It depends on the phase. We have not verified build years for each section, so check your closing paperwork or ask your HOA, and use that date to judge the age of the appliances and the water heater.

What if the water came through a shared townhome wall?

Tell your neighbor and your HOA right away and ask what the association's master policy covers. We can dry the wet materials on your side and document the readings.

Does the Town or my HOA fix a leak under my slab?

No. A leak in a private line is the homeowner's to repair with a plumber. We handle the water damage that results, and the slab leak water damage page explains how those cases run.
