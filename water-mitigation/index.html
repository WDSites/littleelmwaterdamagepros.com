When standing water is an emergency

If water is spreading through your home right now, treat it as an emergency, because wet drywall and subfloor begin to break down fast. Our 24/7 dispatch line is (972) 945-2885, with same-day response when available. While you wait, close the main water shutoff, lift valuables off wet floors, and keep people and pets out of any water that could be contaminated.

Turn off breakers for the wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping through water. Photos and a short video can wait until the source is stopped.

Stay out when water meets electricity or sewage

If water is touching an electrical panel, outlets or a ceiling fixture, or you smell gas, leave the area. Call the electric or gas utility or 911 first, and call the restoration line after that.

If the water came up through a floor drain or toilet, it is sewage. Do not wade in it, do not try to wet-vac it, and keep children and pets away until trained crews with protective gear handle it.

Ceiling stain spreading, carpet soaking, wall feels damp?

A brown ring that grows on the ceiling, carpet that squishes underfoot, baseboards that look swollen or a wall that feels cool and clammy all point to the same thing: moisture is trapped in building materials. The trade term for what comes next is water mitigation, which means limiting the loss by removing the water, drying the structure and measuring the result.

What happens in the first 24 to 72 hours

  1. Stop the source.
  2. Assess the loss. Crews check which materials are wet and which water category the source belongs to under ANSI/IICRC S500.
  3. Extract. Extraction units pull standing water off hard floors and carpet.
  4. Remove what cannot be saved. Soaked pad and saturated drywall at the floor line often come out early.
  5. Set up drying. Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and, where needed, wall cavity drying equipment go in and run around the clock.
  6. Map and monitor. Readings start on day one and are repeated at each visit until the targets are met.

Drying equipment in the first days

Drying starts inside the first 24 to 72 hours, with air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture readings on the affected walls and floors. The equipment keeps running and the doors stay closed until the readings show the materials are dry. The science behind it, including water classes and how a crew decides a job is done, has its own page on structural drying and dehumidification.

Moisture mapping and documentation

Moisture mapping means measuring and recording how wet each material is, then marking those points on a floor plan or photo log. A pin or pinless moisture meter reads drywall, wood and subfloor. A thermal imaging camera shows cold spots that suggest moisture behind a surface, but it only guides the meter.

Readings are repeated on every visit, so you see numbers fall from day to day. When the same spots read at your dry reference level on two checks in a row, drying is verified and the equipment can leave. Ask for a copy of the log and photos for any claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle water mitigation emergencies at night or on weekends in Little Elm?

Yes. A line that lets go at 2 a.m. on a Sunday still reaches a live dispatcher. The first call sorts out what to shut off, and a crew is sent with extraction units and drying gear for the morning work. Same-day response is offered when available.

How long does it take to dry a house after a leak?

Structural drying commonly takes about three to five days, but it depends on the materials, the amount of water and the weather. Hardwood, thick subfloors and wet insulation can add days. The honest answer comes from daily moisture readings, not a calendar.

What drying equipment will be in my house, and why is it so loud?

Expect air movers, which are low, wide fans, and LGR dehumidifiers that pull water out of the air. Some jobs add a cavity drying system for walls. The noise is the machines working, and turning them off to sleep slows the job.

My ceiling has a spreading stain but the pipe stopped dripping. Is it still an emergency?

Often yes. A stain that keeps growing means insulation and drywall above it are still holding water. Shut off water to the suspected line if you can, place a bucket under any drip, and call so readings can be taken before the drywall sags.

Is mitigation the same as repair?

No. Mitigation stops the damage and dries the structure. Repair rebuilds finishes like drywall, flooring and paint, and it starts after drying is verified. You can read about that stage on our water damage repair page.
