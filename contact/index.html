Have This Ready When You Call

  1. The address where the water is, with the nearest cross streets.
  2. Where the water came from, and whether it is still running.
  3. Which rooms and materials are wet, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall or ceiling.
  4. How long the water has been there, as best you know.
  5. Whether you have photos, and the name of your insurer if you plan to file a claim.

While You Wait

Stop the source if you safely can by closing the main water valve. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. Move valuables and furniture out of the water, and keep children and pets away from standing water, especially if it may contain sewage.

Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will want them, and so will the crew. Do not make permanent repairs until your insurance company has seen the damage.

Calling For A Rental, Business Or Relative

You do not have to own the property to call. Tell the dispatcher who you are and who can approve work, whether that is an owner, a property manager or an association board. For rentals, tenants should also notify the landlord quickly, since lease terms and insurance often require it.

For a business, mention whether customers or employees are in the space and whether utilities are on. It changes the order of work and the equipment that goes on the truck.

What Happens After The Call

The dispatcher confirms the address, explains what the crew will do and arranges same-day response when available. When the crew arrives they inspect, take moisture readings and explain the scope and price before work starts. You decide whether to proceed.

Keep your photos, receipts and any notes about the damage. If you plan to file a claim, leave the permanent fixes alone until the adjuster has looked things over.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is there no contact form?

Water damage is time sensitive, and a dispatcher needs to ask about the source, the materials and any safety risk. A phone call does that faster than a form, so every request is taken by phone.

Can I email you instead?

You can write to contact@littleelmwaterdamagepros.com, but email is not monitored like the dispatch line. For active water, call.

Do I need to be home when the crew arrives?

Someone with access to the property should be there. The dispatcher will explain what to leave accessible and what to photograph first.

What if the water is near electrical outlets?

Stay out of the area. Shut off power only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water, and call the electric utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.
