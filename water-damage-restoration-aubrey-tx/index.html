The leak you cannot find is the common one

On larger lots in a fast-growing small town, the usual call is not a dramatic burst. It is a line you cannot find, such as an irrigation run leaking under turf, a supply line to a detached workshop or a slow drip under a slab. You see a wet patch, a jump in the water bill or a soft spot in the yard.

Start with a simple test. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check the meter if you are on city water. If the dial or leak indicator still moves, water is escaping somewhere past the meter. Close the main valve and call us with what you found.

Ranch-style outbuildings, barns with wash racks, pool houses and guest quarters may each have their own supply line and shutoff. Walk the property, list every one and close them in order. Write the list on a card inside the pantry door so anyone in the household can follow it.

Hidden leaks cost more than visible ones because they run for weeks. Insurers tend to treat slow seepage and the mold it feeds differently from a sudden break, so a drip you ignored may be yours to pay for. Ask your agent how your policy treats a leak you did not know about.

A hidden leak calls for tracing before drying. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find the wet path, and then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry it while extraction units clear any pooled water. If water touches an outlet, a panel or a pump, stay back and call the utility or 911 first.

Barn and shop floors matter here too. Concrete wash bays and tack rooms hold water in low spots, and stored feed, bedding or cardboard act like sponges. Move them to dry ground before the crew arrives, and keep animals out of the wet area.

City water or private well? Confirm before you call

Some addresses with an Aubrey, Texas mailing address sit outside city limits and are served by Denton ISD (Braswell High School), while Aubrey ISD serves the town itself. Water providers vary by address. Ask whether your address is inside city limits and whether it is on city water or a private well.

That answer decides your first move. City water has a meter you can read and a utility to call. A private well has a pressure tank and pump on their own breaker, and a leak may show as a pump that cycles constantly.

When you call, tell dispatch which side of the city line you are on and give the road name from your own deed or mail rather than a landmark. Crews drive in from the south, and a precise address saves the time a wrong turn on a rural road would cost.

How do I know if a leak is in my yard?

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch your meter. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house, and a wet patch, green strip or soft ground can show where. Close the main and call us.

Is water damage from a slow leak covered by insurance?

Often not. Policies lean toward sudden, accidental damage but often exclude gradual leaks and seepage. Ask your insurer, and report hidden damage within days after you first see it.
