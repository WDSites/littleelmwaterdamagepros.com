When sewage is coming up through a drain right now

If waste water is rising in a tub, shower, toilet or floor drain, treat it as an emergency and call us now. While you wait, stop running water anywhere in the house. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher. Keep children and pets out of the room, and leave the mess where it is.

Cut power to the affected rooms only if the panel is in a dry spot you can reach without stepping in anything. Photograph the damage first. A crew can often be sent the same day, including after hours.

Industry practice sorts this water by source. ANSI/IICRC S500 defines Category 3 as grossly contaminated water, and sewage and rising flood water both land there. That label matters because it decides what can be saved and what must be bagged.

Gurgling drains, a sewer smell and water in the tub

A tub that fills when the washer drains, a toilet that bubbles when you run the sink, or a floor drain that weeps brown water all point the same direction. Waste water has nowhere to go. The trade term for what comes next is sewage backup cleanup, and it follows containment rules that ordinary water cleanup does not.

Sewage safety: what to do and what to avoid

  • Stay out of the affected area unless you must cross it, and never wade through standing sewage in bare feet or sandals.
  • Do not run a household wet vacuum on it. The exhaust throws fine droplets into the air and the tank becomes contaminated.
  • Keep bare hands off wet materials. Waterproof gloves and boots are the minimum.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone who is unwell out of the rooms until cleanup is finished.
  • Wash hands and forearms with soap and clean water after any contact, and change out of wet clothing before touching other surfaces.

We make no health claims here. Any question about symptoms or exposure belongs to a physician, and severe symptoms mean calling 911.

Electrical, gas and who to call first

If water is touching outlets, a breaker panel or an appliance cord, stay out and do not touch a switch. Gas odor or sparks mean everyone gets out of the house. Phone the utility or 911 about any shock or gas danger before you phone us.

How we clean up after a sewage backup

  1. Containment barriers and sealed doorways keep the affected rooms apart from the rest of the house.
  2. Crews dress in waterproof gloves and boots, coveralls, eye protection and respirators before entering.
  3. Extraction units remove the standing water, and porous materials such as carpet, pad, low drywall and insulation are cut out and bagged.
  4. Hard surfaces, framing and sealed concrete are cleaned, then treated with an antimicrobial.
  5. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what stays, while moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras confirm the readings.
  6. HEPA air scrubbers run through the job to pull odor and fine particles from the air, and all waste is bagged and disposed of properly.

Outside flood water is covered on our flood damage restoration page, and general cleanup fits under water damage cleanup. We do not clear drains or repair sewer lines, so a plumber handles the line itself.

Insurance and sewer backup coverage

Coverage for sewer or drain backup is often sold as a separate endorsement on a home policy, not included by default. Ask your agent whether yours has one. Check your policy for how it treats backup, plumbing failure and outside flood water, since each can be handled differently.

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says to report a claim as soon as possible and to keep damaged items until the adjuster sees them. In practice that means photographing before you bag anything and asking your insurer what it wants held. We never promise a claim outcome.

Mistakes that make a backup worse

  • Running the washer or flushing to see whether the line has cleared.
  • Mopping or wet vacuuming sewage yourself, which spreads it into dry rooms.
  • Throwing out ruined items before photographing them.
  • Waiting a day to see if it drains.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Little Elm on a Saturday night?

Yes. Holidays and weekends are covered, and same-day visits are possible depending on the schedule, including nights and weekends. Call first, stay out of the affected rooms, and keep everyone away from the water until the crew arrives.

Why is my floor drain bubbling and my toilet gurgling?

Gurgling usually means air is being pushed back through your drains because the line is blocked. When more than one fixture does it, the blockage is probably at the main line or past your property. A plumber can scope the line, and we handle the cleanup if water reaches the floor.

Who do I call if the blockage is on the Town side?

Ask the Town of Little Elm, which bills water and solid waste to residents, who to call for a public line. We do not have a Town number to give you. Meanwhile, keep water use down and call us if sewage is already inside.

Does my carpet have to be thrown away?

Usually yes. Carpet and pad that soaked up sewage are porous and are removed and bagged. Tile and sealed concrete can generally be cleaned and treated.
