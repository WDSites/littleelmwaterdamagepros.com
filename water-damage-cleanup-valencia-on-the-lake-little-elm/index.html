A lakeside community still being built

Valencia on the Lake is a Centurion American master-planned community of about 448 acres along Rockhill Parkway, on the shores of Lewisville Lake, with more than 80 acres of green space according to a builder listing service. Several homebuilders sell here, the Town of Little Elm lists the Valencia on the Lake HOA in its HOA directory, and the homes are in the Little Elm ISD.

Listing data shows homes still being sold, which means some blocks have settled in while others are new or under construction. We have not verified build years for individual streets, so ask your builder or your HOA when yours was finished.

What the lake does and does not tell you

Lewisville Dam, finished in 1955 and run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, holds the lake at a normal pool of 522 feet with a spillway crest at 532 feet. In June 2015 the lake climbed to 537 feet after record May rain. That was a lake-wide event, and we have not found any record tying it to damage in this community.

Treat it as a lesson in how the lake behaves and not as a history of Valencia. Neighborhood-level FEMA flood-zone status is unverified on our side, so look up your own address on the FEMA map and ask your insurer whether a separate flood policy fits.

Plumbing leak or flood: the insurance difference

Texas Department of Insurance guidance describes sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a broken washer hose, as usually covered, and floods and gradual seepage as usually not. A leaking supply line and rising lake water can look alike on a floor, but they are treated differently. Call your insurer before you assume either way.

Safety first when the water may be outside water

Stay out of flood water

Water that came from outside is treated as contaminated. Keep people and pets out, and do not wade through it to reach a panel.

Electricity

Do not touch outlets or cords while water is near. If the panel is dry and reachable, a breaker can go off, and otherwise leave it.

Gas

Any gas smell means leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

Standing water outside the house

Do not drive or walk through high water around the neighborhood to reach a shutoff.

Drying a lakeside home

Outside water is treated as Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage standard, until it has been assessed. That usually means more material removed, antimicrobial treatment and containment barriers so cleanup does not spread soil to dry rooms. Extraction units come first and LGR dehumidifiers and air movers follow, with moisture readings logged until the structure is dry.

Our flood damage restoration page explains the stages, storm water damage cleanup covers wind-driven rain and emergency water removal explains the pumping step.

Rain through the roof or a window

A ceiling stain after a storm is a different problem from a lakeshore event. Find where the opening is, ask your insurer what it treats as storm damage, and protect the interior with a tarp or cover only where you can do so safely. A roofer fixes the roof and we dry what got wet, as described on our roof leak water damage page.

Documenting the loss

Shoot photos or video before anything moves, write down what was damaged, save the receipts and keep ruined items for the adjuster. Texas Department of Insurance guidance says not to make permanent repairs until the insurer has seen the damage. Moisture readings can support your record, but coverage is your insurer's decision. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Valencia on the Lake in Little Elm?

Yes. The Town of Little Elm lists the Valencia on the Lake HOA in its HOA directory, and builder listings put the community along Rockhill Parkway beside Lewisville Lake.

Is my home in a FEMA flood zone?

We have not verified flood-zone status for the neighborhood as a whole. FEMA maps work address by address, so look yours up on the FEMA map and ask your insurer or agent how it applies.

Does my homeowners policy cover lake flooding?

Home policies exclude floods according to Texas Department of Insurance guidance, so flood water falls under a separate flood policy. Talk to your agent before an event, not after one.

Is storm water treated differently from a pipe leak?

Yes. Water from outside is treated as contaminated until assessed, so it takes more containment and removal than clean supply water. Both still need drying with moisture readings.
