When flood water is an emergency in your home

If water is coming into your house right now, treat it as an emergency, because our 24/7 dispatch line exists for exactly this moment. While you wait, stop the source if you can reach it, shut off the main water valve when a pipe is involved, and lift valuables, papers and electronics off the floor. Keep people and pets out of the water. Storm and creek water carries soil, fuel and bacteria.

Kill the breakers for the flooded rooms only if the panel sits in a dry spot you can reach without getting wet. Snap a photo of the water line first, since your insurer will ask. We offer same-day response when available, day or night.

Safety first: electricity, gas and contaminated water

Water around a breaker panel, outlets or wired stairwell means nobody goes in and nobody touches a switch. A gas smell or sparks means everyone leaves the house. Make that call first, to the power company, the gas company or 911, and phone us once the hazard has been reported.

Do not wade through water that may hold sewage or runoff.

Flood water by category: what it means for the plan

ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into three categories by source, and the category sets the plan.

CategoryTypical flood sourceWhat it meansWhat is usually done
Category 1Broken supply line, clean source caught earlyClean at the start but it degrades if left wetExtract, dry in place, track moisture with meters
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, sump overflowGray water with some contaminationExtract, pull wet pad and low drywall as needed, clean and dry

Outdoor flood water is Category 3 the moment it enters, even groundwater pushing up at a slab joint. It is rarely clean, and wet materials left in place invite mold, so speed matters.

Lower levels and walkouts

Many North Texas homes sit on slabs, so true basements are uncommon here. Any lower level that took water, such as a walkout or a below-grade room, starts with pumping, then removing soaked carpet and drywall above the wicking line, then drying. If water returns after every heavy rain, that is a drainage question for another trade. Our basement water damage cleanup page covers lower levels in detail.

When sewage is involved

Sewage from a drain, tub or toilet is Category 3 water. Stay out, keep children and pets away, do not use a wet vacuum on it, and call 911 for severe symptoms. Crews work in protective gear behind containment barriers and remove porous materials. The full process is on our sewage backup cleanup page.

Whole-house flooding

When water covers most of the first floor, the job becomes a project. Crews pull flooring and baseboards, then cut drywall above the wicking line. Our water mitigation page explains the drying stage that follows.

A wet crawl space under a raised floor is a separate case, covered on our crawl space water damage cleanup page. Wet framing that stays wet is also where mold starts, so drying comes first and mold remediation follows only if growth has set in.

Flood insurance: what your home policy does not cover

Most homeowners and renters policies pay for sudden, accidental water such as a burst pipe, yet rising water from outside is excluded. Covering a flood means buying a flood policy of its own. Read your declarations page before the storm, not after.

Where your address sits matters. Pull up the FEMA flood map and see whether your address sits in a mapped zone. Tell the adjuster whether the water came from outside, a plumbing failure or a sewer line, since the cause shapes which policy responds.

Claim paperwork goes smoother with a record. Shoot video of every room, save every receipt, and set aside ruined items for the adjuster. Report the loss quickly and hold off on rebuilding until your insurer has seen it. We never promise a claim outcome.

Living on a flood-control lake: Lewisville Lake and your address

Little Elm sits on the northern and eastern shores of Lewisville Lake. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers has operated the dam since its completion in 1955. Normal pool is 522 feet and the spillway crest is 532 feet. Your own lot may sit higher or lower than the shoreline suggests.

In June 2015, after record May rainfall and a tropical storm, the lake reached 537 feet, five feet above the spillway crest. Little Elm Park's beach and central recreation area went underwater. If your home was here then, pull old photos and claim paperwork, since they show how water behaved around your lot.

A multi-year Army Corps of Engineers dam safety upgrade is scheduled for completion in 2026, with anchors drilled about 180 feet deep and seepage monitoring. Use it as a prompt to review your flood coverage, as neighbors in The Colony with about 23 miles of shoreline should too. May (4.78 inches) and October (4.37) are the wettest months at DFW, so clear gutters before them. Homes beside the lake can read how that plays out in Valencia on the Lake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Little Elm?

Yes. Storms do not keep office hours, so the line stays staffed around the clock, Saturdays, Sundays and holidays included. Tell the dispatcher where the water is and what it came from, and you will get first steps over the phone while a crew heads out from our Little Elm base, same-day when available.

How long does a flooded home take to dry?

It depends on the water category, the materials and the weather. Many structures need several days with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and moisture meter readings, not the calendar, decide when it is done.

Does homeowners insurance pay for flood damage?

Not under a standard home policy, which is why the earlier insurance section matters. A burst pipe inside the house is judged differently from water coming in from outside, so the source counts. Ask your insurer.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Not while standing water, damaged wiring or sewage is in the living area. Stay out of wet rooms and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk.

Will mold grow after a flood?

Mold needs moisture, and wet drywall and framing supply it. Fast drying lowers the chance, and mold remediation applies only if growth has started. Nobody can promise it will not appear.
