What We Do

Little Elm Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 dispatch service for water damage restoration and mold remediation in Little Elm, Texas, and the nearby towns of Frisco, The Colony, Prosper and Aubrey. When you call, a dispatcher listens to what happened, asks a few questions about the water and the materials, and sends licensed and insured restoration professionals.

We keep the model simple on purpose. One number, one conversation, and a plain explanation of scope and price before anyone starts work. This site is for people with water on the floor, and it is written for them.

How A Call Goes

The dispatcher first asks whether anyone is at risk, because electrical hazards, sewage and gas come before restoration. Next come the basics: where the water came from, whether it is still running, which rooms are wet and how long it has been there.

From there the dispatcher explains what a crew would do, such as extraction, drying equipment or containment for mold, and arranges same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time. Distance, weather and call volume all move it, and an honest estimate beats a promise that gets missed.

Standards We Point To

The restoration industry publishes two standards that homeowners can ask about by name. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers professional mold remediation. Ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to them.

Texas also licenses mold assessment and mold remediation through the Department of Licensing and Regulation, and the two roles cannot be filled by the same licensee on one project. When you hire for mold work, ask whether the person holds the license the state requires. The mold remediation page explains the process.

What We Do Not Do

We do not repair roofs, replace plumbing or fix foundations. If a pipe failed or a roof leaked, the cause needs its own trade, and the restoration crew will point it out. We do not sell insurance, we do not decide claims, and we do not promise that a claim will be approved.

We also do not publish customer reviews or star ratings on this site. You can judge us by the clarity of the scope we give you and by what the dispatcher tells you on the phone.

Why A Dispatch Line Instead Of A Showroom

Most water damage calls come at the worst possible time, such as a Saturday night or a holiday morning. A staffed dispatch line means a person answers, not a voicemail, and the crew can be lined up while you are still on the phone. The trade-off is that we are not a storefront, and there is no walk-in counter.

Our base on East Eldorado Parkway keeps us tied to the town we write about. We know the lake, the main roads and the way Little Elm has grown, and we say what we do not know instead of guessing.

What We Ask Of You

Be ready to describe the damage, photograph it before cleanup and tell the dispatcher about any safety risk. Ask questions. A good restoration company expects them, and a scope you understand is a scope you can compare with any other.

What Kinds Of Jobs Come Through

Calls range from a supply line that failed under a sink to a water heater that let go in a garage, a slab leak that showed up as warm flooring, or a storm that pushed water through a door threshold. Some are small and contained. Others involve several rooms, wet insulation and a ceiling that has started to sag.

Mold calls usually arrive a little later, once a musty smell or a dark patch appears behind furniture or under a sink. The dispatcher sorts these by urgency, so an active leak is handled before a stain that has been dry for weeks.

How We Handle Hard Questions

Some questions have no clean answer over the phone, such as how long drying will take or whether a wall can be saved. The honest reply is that it depends on moisture readings taken on site. We would rather say that than guess and be wrong.

If a request falls outside water damage and mold, such as a fire loss, we say so and suggest you look for a company that does that work.
